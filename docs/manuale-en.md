# QTHome — manual

QTHome is the program for the whole station: radio, log, CW, digital modes, cluster, SDR, satellites, contests and more, in one window. It is one program: the radio, your callsign, the current QSO and the log are the same in every section. Listening and decoding are free.

## Installing

- **Mac**: download `QTHome-Mac.pkg`, open it and follow the steps. QTHome is in Applications.
- **Windows**: download `QTHome-Windows.zip`, open it and drag `QTHome.exe` wherever you like (for example the Desktop), then open it.

QTHome updates itself: when there is a new version it downloads it and installs it when you quit.

## The first time

1. Connect the radio to the computer with the USB cable and turn it on.
2. Open QTHome: the **welcome window** explains the first steps (tick «Don't show again» when you no longer need it), then it asks for your **callsign and locator**. Every function uses them.
3. The radio appears by itself. In **Station** the **Getting started** card shows what is missing (radio, audio, cluster), with a button to do it.
4. On Mac, the first time you listen to the radio, allow the **microphone** for QTHome: it is the audio coming from the radio.
5. For another language: **Settings › Appearance, language and accessibility**.

## The window

- **At the top**: the menus, the state of the radio and of the SDR dongle, your callsign and UTC time. **Waterfall**: the «Adjust» button next to every waterfall changes automatic level, brightness, contrast, colours and height.
- **The radio bar** is always visible: radio, band, frequency (VFO A and B), mode, filter, **PTT · TX** (it turns red whenever the radio transmits, whoever keys it; it releases itself after 3 minutes) and **Radio controls** with every control of the front panel.
- **On the left** the sections. By default you see only the essential ones; the others are under **+ More functions** (or always all of them: **Settings › Appearance, language and accessibility**).
- **At the bottom, the waterfall**, above the QSO bar, in Station, Log, Phone, Contest, Cluster, Map, Satellites and Rotator: one click on «Waterfall» folds it.
- **Can't find something?** ⌘K (Ctrl+K on Windows) opens the search: type “SSTV”, “rotator”, “HamQTH” or “licence” and press Enter.
- **At the bottom the QSO bar**: the current QSO, one for the whole program. Type the callsign (Alt+Q gets there from anywhere), reports, name and locator; frequency, band and mode come from the radio. It tells you the country, distance, bearing, which bands he is heard on right now and whether you have worked him. **Enter** saves to the log, **Esc** starts over. While transmitting, **Esc** stops all transmission (FT8, phone, CW, PTT), like Station › Stop all transmission. In the note you can write "POTA IT-0123", "SOTA I/LO-123" or "WWFF IFF-0123": the reference goes into its log field. Saved by mistake? Right after, **Undo** appears: the QSO leaves the log and goes back into the bar. If you are in Europe or Africa, it warns you when you are on phone in the CW/digital part of the band or on 30 m (IARU band plan).
- Without the radio connected (or with the **✎** button) you type frequency, mode, date and UTC time by hand: useful to log a QSO made with a portable rig.
- The cluster, the map, "You can work now", FT8, RTTY/PSK and CW also fill the current QSO: a click on a spot takes the radio to the right frequency and mode and puts the callsign in the bar. With a rotator, **↻ Rotator** turns the antenna towards him.
- Everything works from the keyboard and with a screen reader. For blind or low-vision users: **Settings › Appearance, language and accessibility › Turn on accessibility** (spoken announcements, Morse, S-meter read with ⌘⇧S). It is off by default.

## The sections

- **Station**: the big frequency, S-meter, PTT, radio controls, memories (also from and to CHIRP), who you can work now and open bands. **S-meter in dBm**: in Settings › Radios and programs it reads in S units and dBm and can be calibrated with a known signal. **FlexRadio radios** (SmartSDR) found automatically on the home network. **Nearby repeaters** from RepeaterBook (file exported from the site), one click to memory; **Write to the radio** loads the memories into Icom radios via CI-V (other radios and handhelds with CHIRP). **USB paddle** (Settings › Accessories): iambic keyer that keys the radio with the DTR line of its USB port; MIDI paddles too («Learn the paddles»: press the dot, then the dash). **From your phone**: in Settings › Radios and programs › Phone you turn on a web app for the home network, with a PIN (frequency, band, mode, spots, log and new QSOs; no transmitting from the phone).
- **Log**: every QSO, from every section. Search by callsign, country or band; **click a row** to correct or delete it. Paper QSLs (bureau and direct, labels to print) and **electronic QSLs**: LoTW (needs TQSL, free), eQSL, QRZ Logbook and Club Log, HRDLog.net, Wavelog/Cloudlog. Awards and station profiles. If you activate a park or summit, put the reference in the profile (Profiles… › “Activating”): every QSO carries it in the log and in ADIF; in Digital modes the menu next to CQ calls “CQ POTA” or “CQ SOTA”. **Statistics…**: QSOs by year, band, mode and UTC hour, the best day and the confirmed ones. In the profile also put the **power** (W): it goes into every QSO (TX_PWR), picks the power category in contests, and Statistics show the km-per-watt record. Exporting by profile gives the file named the way POTA and WWFF want it. In **Awards…** the “only those still to confirm” box lists what you worked without a confirmation, with the callsigns to ask for a QSL. Among the awards there is ARI's **WAIP** (Italian provinces): the province comes from the QSO (province field when editing the QSO), the ARI contest exchange or “WAIP-XX” in the note. Awards also include **WCA** (castles), **ILLW** (lighthouses) and **JCC** (Japanese cities): the reference comes from the ADIF SIG/SIG_INFO or the note (“WCA I-00123”).
- **Phone**: listening and recording, the last 60 seconds always savable, 4 voice memories (F1-F4). **FreeDV** (700D, 700E, 700C, 1600): listen from the radio audio and «Hold to talk». **Pi-Star / WPSD**: who comes through your DMR, D-STAR or YSF hotspot, one click into the QSO bar.
- **CW**: the CWEngine decoder, keyer, messages, the skimmer with the SDR dongle. Once on it keeps working while you use other sections. QSOs made here go into the QTHome log. CW decoding is free forever.
- **Digital modes**: FT8 and FT4 like WSJT-X (auto sequence, CQ, auto log), RTTY, PSK31/63, Feld Hell, QRSS (also DFCW), APRS, WSPR, Q65, JT65 and MSK144 (without installing WSJT-X) and SSTV (images: the mode is recognised automatically, received images stay in the gallery). The band follows the radio. FT8, FT4, RTTY and PSK spots put the radio in the right place and open Digital modes with the station ready. Decodes and QSOs can also go to GridTracker, JTAlert, N1MM or Log4OM, as from WSJT-X (Settings › Radios and programs). With sending on, a double click on a station in GridTracker or JTAlert sets it up in Digital modes (Tx1 ready, you decide when to transmit). In automatic hunting it answers only CQs meant for you: “CQ POTA/SOTA/WWFF” always (portables count again every day), “CQ DX” only if the caller is on another continent, “CQ NA”, “CQ EU”… only if it is yours, “CQ TEST” only with a contest running. **WEFAX**: weather charts from coastal stations (DWD Hamburg, Northwood), straightened and saved automatically. **NAVTEX**: navigational warnings and marine weather (518 and 490 kHz), with messages grouped by subject; every character arrives twice and is rebuilt even if one copy is damaged. **Olivia and Contestia** (every tones/bandwidth combination, receive and transmit). **THOR** (4, 5, 8, 11, 16, 22): tuning is not critical and it resists multipath.
- **Contest**: ARI DX, CQ WW, CQ WPX, IARU HF, VHF and generic; serials, dupes, Cabrillo and SO2R. Enter logs, Esc clears. With a contest started, the QSO bar also asks for the received exchange and every QSO (FT8, CW, RTTY…) goes into the contest; the running contest is shown at the top. The score follows the contest rules (CQ WW, WPX, ARI DX, IARU, VHF) and goes into the Cabrillo; in CQ WW and IARU the zone is filled in from the prefix. Contester tools: band map, callsign suggestions as you type (with the one-character “maybe”), editable F1-F4 messages ({MYCALL} {CALL} {EXCH}) and ESM, Enter that sends CQ, exchange and TU. There is also the ARI FT8 Activity Contest (IAC): 1 point per QSO, multipliers are 4-character squares. Without a connected radio (no CAT) choose band and mode by hand next to the callsign. There is also the **ARRL International DX** for stations outside the USA and Canada (3 points per W/VE station, states and provinces as multipliers). At the top, the **upcoming big contests** with their dates: a click selects it; your exchange (zone, locator, power) is filled in by itself, and a reminder arrives the day before.
- **Satellites and space**: next passes, Doppler, tracking with the rotator and radio correction. Orbits download by themselves. QO-100: pointing from your locator, LNB skew, beacons and conversion between downlink, uplink and LNB output.
- **Cluster and propagation**: DX cluster and Reverse Beacon Network with filters; a click on a spot takes the radio there. Solar data and band conditions. NCDXF/IARU beacons: who is transmitting now on each band, distance and bearing, and which ones were really heard from your continent. “Open now”: band by continent, with the stations really heard from your continent in the last 30 minutes. You can add POTA, SOTA and WWFF portable spots: the park or summit reference goes into the log. **24-hour forecast**: which bands open, hour by hour, towards the QSO station or an area of the world; ITU-R P.533 model with the CCIR maps (like VOACAP, built into QTHome) and the reliability of each band with the station noise (ITU-R P.372) (the “Open now” spots tell what is really open). The QSO bar shows it when there are no spots towards that station. In **Show** choose only new countries or only stations you have not yet worked on that band. DXCC countries update by themselves every week. **Rays in the ionosphere**: below the forecast, the ray paths by band, number of hops and time (skip zone, take-off angles that reach your partner).
- **Map**: night and greyline, spots, who hears you, EME, Es; a click on a spot works it. "My QSOs" shows where the stations in the log are (green = confirmed). With “Open from you (estimate)” pick a band and the map colours the parts of the world that band reaches now from your station.
- **SDR**: receiver with the RTL-SDR dongle, spectrum and waterfall; click to listen to a frequency. The dongle also receives **ships (AIS)**, on the Map and to OpenCPN as NMEA, and **aircraft (ACARS)**. And **RS41 radiosondes** (400-406 MHz): position, altitude and speed, with the track on the Map. **Meteor-M weather satellite images (LRPT, 137.9 and 137.1 MHz)**: in colour during a pass, saved automatically in the lrpt folder. **Radio astronomy**: radiometer with the dongle (power over time and average spectrum, CSV file): Jupiter at 20.1 MHz, Sun and Moon, the hydrogen line at 1420.4 MHz with velocity in km/s. **Aircraft (ADS-B)**: aircraft at 1090 MHz on the Map and, with your partner's locator, those useful for aircraft scatter on 2 m, 70 cm and 23 cm (distances, azimuth and elevation).
- **Rotator**: point it by degrees, towards a locator or towards the current QSO, also long path.
- **QTHome Chat**: text and voice rooms between hams, encrypted private messages; nothing stays on the server. **APRS over the internet (APRS-IS)**: APRS messages with your callsign and nearby APRS stations on the Map (light blue). **AREDN mesh network**: with the computer connected to a node, reachable nodes, network services and link quality. **AX.25 packet radio** (terminal for BBSs and nodes, with Direwolf or a TNC) and **Meshtastic** (LoRa messages, nodes on the Map). **EchoLink**: connect to EchoLink repeaters, links and stations worldwide straight from QTHome, with no other program (station directory, voice, text); you need a callsign validated by EchoLink and UDP ports 5198-5199 open in your router.
- **Winlink**: mail over the internet or by radio with the VARA modem (installed separately): inbox, outbox, attachments, ICS-213 form for emergencies. Your Winlink password stays on your computer.
- **Tools**: calculators, Smith chart, antenna lab, pile-up training, **learn CW with the Koch method**, licence quiz. **NanoVNA**: SWR, impedance and 2:1 bandwidth of the antenna, with a tip on whether to shorten or lengthen it. **Transmitter linearity**: two-tone test measured with the SDR dongle (IMD 3rd, 5th, 7th), into a dummy load.
- **Connections**: everything QTHome uses, each with its light (green = working) and its button: the radios (more than one too, and FlexRadio on the network), the SDR dongle, which radio and audio each function uses, external programs (WSJT-X, fldigi, JS8Call, loggers: they connect through QTHome, all together), decodes to GridTracker and similar, the phone, accessories (transmit sequencer, amplifier, antenna switch, battery, weather, WinKeyer, USB paddle). You also get there by clicking the lights at the top. At the top of each function a strip shows whether it is on and which radio it uses. **SO2R** (Connections): interlock so that only one radio transmits at a time, and SO2R box with the OTRSP protocol (antennas and microphone to the transmitting radio, headphones to the radio chosen in Contest). **Bluetooth** radios (IC-705, Bluetooth CAT adapters) are paired in the system first: they become a serial port and QTHome finds them like USB ones.
- **Settings**: your station, transverter and S-meter, appearance, language and accessibility. In **My station** you also choose the **bands and modes you use** (“You can work now” and the cluster follow them) and the **alerts**: new country on the air, satellite coming and “you are heard” (your callsign in spots), in any panel.

**Kiosk mode** (Settings › Appearance): for fairs, club rooms and demonstrations. Only listening functions remain; settings and transmission disappear (blocked in the engine, also from external programs). You leave with the PIN chosen when turning it on.

**Phone** (Connections › Phone): turn on «Use QTHome from your phone», open the address in the phone's browser and type the PIN. With «Also away from home» it works anywhere over the internet, without touching the router: the program connects to the QTHome service by itself and gives you an address to keep secret. From the phone: frequency, mode, spots, log; never transmitting.

**Help improve CW** (Settings): while you listen to CW, two QTHome decoders reread what arrives and the callsigns are checked against the Reverse Beacon Network; only doubtful cases are sent, anonymously.

**Cloud log** (Connections › Cloud log, with the PRO licence): the log stays on your computer and the QTHome cloud keeps a copy that keeps all your computers in step: a QSO made portable shows up at home, a correction or a deletion reaches everywhere (the most recent change wins). Without internet you work as usual and it catches up when the network is back. «Open the log in a browser» shows it from any computer or phone, with the licence. **Club log**: whoever holds the licence for the club callsign creates the club key and gives it to members; every QTHome using it writes and reads the same log, and the Operator field (from the station profile) says who made the QSO. The cloud copy is private but not encrypted, so it can be read in a browser.

## Free and PRO

Listening, decoding and logging are always free. Transmitting from QTHome, automation, advanced contesting and online QSLs are part of PRO; the first 30 days are a full trial. PRO costs **€12 a year**: it covers server and research costs.

Terms of use, privacy and refund within 14 days: Help › Terms of use and privacy.

## If something goes wrong

- **Check the station**: **Help › Check the station…** tests callsign, radio, audio, clock, internet, licence and updates in one go, with a button to fix each problem.
- **The radio does not appear**: check the USB cable and that the radio is on; then **Connections › Connected radios › Search for radios again**.
- **No audio**: on Mac, System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone › turn QTHome on. On Windows, Settings › Privacy › Microphone.
- **Report a problem**: **Help › Report a problem or an idea…** It is sent anonymously, without callsigns, names or addresses.
